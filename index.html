01 Text message

The grandparent scam.

“Grandma, I had an accident. I need you to send money right now. Please don’t tell Mom.”
What you’ll learn to spot

Urgency, secrecy, and requests for money from an unfamiliar number—even when the story feels personal.

02 Email

The delivery that needs a fee.

“Your package is on hold. Pay the $2.99 redelivery fee today or your parcel will be returned.”
What you’ll learn to spot

A small payment request and a convincing-looking link designed to get you to enter your card details.

03 Phone call

The “bank” protecting your money.

“Your account has been compromised. Move your savings into this safe account. We’ll stay on the line.”
What you’ll learn to spot

A caller using fear to rush a transfer—and why you should hang up and call your bank on a known number.

Michael’s story

For Michael, family means his mom and his grandparents, Marlene and John. Safekeep’s mission starts with loved ones like them.

Michael’s mom was scammed and lost money. It’s a short sentence for something no family wants to go through. The person on the other end of that scam wasn’t just a name or an account number. She was his mom.

At 32, Michael is helping build Safekeep with a simple hope: that more families can recognize the warning signs before someone they love gets hurt. Because the people who have spent their lives looking out for us deserve someone looking out for them, too.

Tim’s story

For Tim, that circle includes his dad and his grandparents, Fred and Lisa. Helping older adults stay safe is about the people he loves, too.

Tim’s dad has Alzheimer’s. Early in that journey, he was badly scammed. An already difficult chapter for their family now included someone taking advantage of a person they loved.

At 35, Tim is helping build Safekeep because a lifetime of savings represents so much more than a balance. It means years of work, plans for the future, and the freedom to live with dignity. Helping families protect that is deeply personal to him.

A lifetime of hard work deserves protecting.

As AI makes scams more sophisticated and harder to recognize, we want practical, ongoing scam practice to feel approachable, supportive, and part of everyday life. No judgment. Just a little more preparation, together.

Before you get started

Good questions.
Straightforward answers.

How can I help protect elderly parents from scams?

Start with a supportive conversation and practice recognizing suspicious requests together. Safekeep is being developed to provide weekly scam simulations, clear feedback, and monthly reports for subscribers and their chosen family members. It’s scam-awareness education designed to support independence.

Will I learn how to tell if a call is a scam?

The planned simulations will help you recognize warning signs in scam calls targeting seniors, such as pressure to act immediately, keep a request secret, or move money. Practice can help you pause and check a request, but it cannot guarantee a call is safe.

Do I need to be good with technology?

No. The planned simulations use familiar channels: texts, emails, and phone calls. Feedback explains the warning signs in plain language. You can make this page’s text larger using the text-size control.

What happens after a simulation?

The planned program follows each simulated scam with clear feedback: what the warning signs were and how to respond safely. It’s a chance to learn before real money is involved.

Will my family and I receive a monthly report?

Yes. As part of the planned subscription, Safekeep will send you and your chosen family members a monthly report summarizing your simulation performance, including warning signs you recognized and areas to keep practicing. It will also include updates on new and emerging scams and what to watch out for. You decide which family members receive the report.

Is Safekeep a monthly subscription?

Yes. Safekeep is being developed as a monthly subscription for weekly scam simulations, coaching, and monthly family reports. Join the waitlist to hear when enrollment opens. The examples on this page show the kinds of simulations planned for the subscription. Joining the waitlist is free.

How will simulations reach me?

The monthly subscription includes weekly simulations by text message, email, and phone, with feedback after each simulation. Participants will opt in before receiving any simulations. This landing page shows illustrative examples only; it does not send simulations or make calls.

Will you access my bank accounts?

No. Scam practice does not require access to your bank accounts, passwords, or payment codes.

Does practice guarantee I won’t be scammed?

No service can guarantee that. These examples help you recognize common warning signs and practice checking before acting. For a real concern, use the help guide below.
