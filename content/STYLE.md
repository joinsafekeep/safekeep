# Safekeep writing style

Write the way a thoughtful person talks to a friend's parent over coffee: warm, patient, specific, a little informal. The reader is usually 60 or older, or the grown child of someone who is. They're smart and they've seen a lot. They don't need to be dazzled; they need to understand what's happening and feel calmer at the end.

## Sound like a person

- Use full, natural sentences with connective words ("so," "which means," "that's why," "and"). Let sentences run to a normal length. Vary the rhythm the way speech does.
- Use contractions. Say "you'll," "it's," "don't."
- Explain the way you would out loud. If a sentence would sound odd read to someone across a table, rewrite it.
- Show, with a small real-feeling detail, rather than announcing a conclusion. "The call often comes late in the evening, when it's harder to reach anyone to check" beats "Timing is a weapon."
- It's fine to admit limits and uncertainty: "Nobody can promise you'll never be fooled."

## Patterns to avoid

These read as machine-written. Don't use them, even once.

- **Contrast flips:** "It's not X, it's Y." "X, not Y." "This isn't about A. It's about B." "Less X, more Y." "Pressuring you, not helping you."
- **The one thing / the key / the secret:** "the one thing scammers can't fake," "the key is," "here's the thing," "the real problem," "what most people miss."
- **Colon reveals and punchline sentences:** "The takeaway: …" "The bright line: …" "The passcode is theater." "There is never a real hacker."
- **Clipped fragments for effect:** "More than ever recorded." "Because the scams are better." "Especially if they say it's secret."
- **Aphorisms and slogans:** "Rules beat judgment." "One talk fades; a check-in sticks." "A cloned voice can copy how you sound, but not what you know."
- **Compressed, over-efficient phrasing:** strings of short declaratives, every sentence doing one job. Add the small words people actually say.
- **Rule-of-three habits:** not every list needs three items, and not every sentence needs three clauses.
- **Absolutes and overclaims:** "works every time," "always," "never fails," "the only defense."
- **Stock filler:** "In today's world," "It's important to note," "navigate," "landscape," "game-changer," "empower," "peace of mind" (unless quoting the homepage), "crucial," "delve."
- **Bold lead-ins on every bullet.** Use them sparingly, only where a reader is scanning steps.

## Keep

- H2s phrased as the questions people search for, the "Quick answer" paragraph at the top, and the FAQ section. Those are for search engines and AI answers, and they can still be written warmly.
- Every number tied to a named source and a link. Paraphrase sources; never quote more than a short phrase.
- Cite the original source wherever one exists: the FBI/IC3 report or PSA, the FTC report or press release, the DOJ or HHS-OIG page. Link PDFs to the exact page (`…report.pdf#page=44`). Use news coverage only for things the agencies didn't publish themselves, and say whose figure it is.
- Safekeep is open for sign-ups. Don't call the simulations planned or coming soon. Never imply Safekeep guarantees protection.
- No fear-mongering. The tone is calm and on the reader's side.
