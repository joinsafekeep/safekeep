**Quick answer:** Medicare open enrollment runs from October 15 to December 7, 2026, and scam calls pick up right along with it. A good rule of thumb is that Medicare won't call, text, or email you to confirm your Medicare number, send you a new card, or sell you a plan. If a caller asks for your number, offers you something free, or says you'll lose your benefits unless you act today, it's safest to hang up and call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) yourself.

## Why do Medicare scams spike during open enrollment?

During these weeks, real insurance companies and Medicare Advantage plans are reaching out to people too, so the fake calls blend in more easily ([TheStreet](https://thestreet.com/personal-finance/medicare-scam-calls-open-enrollment-2026)). Open enrollment for 2027 coverage began on October 15, 2026, and scammers use the deadline to make people feel rushed, posing as government officials who want to update your card, offer a genetic test, or warn that your coverage is about to be canceled.

Many people started getting these calls well before enrollment opened. By late August, some beneficiaries were reporting Medicare-related calls several times a day, even though they were on the Do Not Call list ([Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/dont-waste-your-money/they-call-15-times-a-day-seniors-inundated-with-medicare-calls-ahead-of-open-enrollment)).

## What are the most common Medicare scam calls?

**1. "We need to confirm your Medicare number."** Medicare already has your number, and it won't call, text, or email asking you to confirm it ([Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/dont-waste-your-money/they-call-15-times-a-day-seniors-inundated-with-medicare-calls-ahead-of-open-enrollment)). Medicare's own advice is to give your Medicare number only to your doctor or others you know should have it ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/reporting-medicare-fraud-and-abuse)). A thief can do a lot with that number, including billing Medicare for care you never got, so it deserves the same caution as your Social Security number.

**2. "Your new or upgraded Medicare card is ready."** If someone calls offering a new, plastic, or "upgraded" card, be suspicious. There's no fee for a Medicare card and no phone call needed to get one.

**3. A "free" genetic test or cheek swab.** These offers come by phone, at health fairs, and sometimes at the front door. What the scammer is really after is your Medicare number, so they can bill for expensive tests you didn't need. If Medicare denies the claim, you could be billed for the full cost, which can run to thousands of dollars ([HHS Office of Inspector General](https://oig.hhs.gov/fraud/consumer-alerts/fraud-alert-genetic-testing-scam/)).

**4. A "free" brace, device, or "Medicare kit."** Calls and texts offering a free back brace, knee brace, or medical kit are a common cover for billing fraud. More than 40 health systems recently warned their patients about a fake "MyChart Medicare Kit" message going around ([Scripps News, citing Becker's Healthcare](https://www.scrippsnews.com/dont-waste-your-money/they-call-15-times-a-day-seniors-inundated-with-medicare-calls-ahead-of-open-enrollment)). If you need medical equipment, your own doctor will order it for you.

**5. "This plan is endorsed by Medicare."** Medicare doesn't endorse or prefer any private plan, and insurance agents aren't allowed to say they represent Medicare. Someone who says either of those things is breaking the rules, or isn't a real agent at all.

**6. "You'll lose your benefits if you don't switch today."** You have until December 7 to make any changes, and if you do nothing, your coverage carries on. Anyone insisting that you decide during the call is trying to rush you.

## How can you tell a real Medicare call from a scam?

It helps to ask yourself a few questions while you're on the phone. Did you contact them first, or did they call you out of the blue? Under Medicare's marketing rules, plans and agents generally aren't allowed to cold-call you unless you've asked them to or you're already a member. Are they asking for your Medicare number, Social Security number, or bank details? And are they hurrying you? A real coverage decision can always wait a day while you check.

If you're unsure, end the call and dial 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) yourself, or compare plans directly at [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/). Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) also offers free, unbiased help choosing a plan.

## What should you do if you gave out your Medicare number?

1. Call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) to report it and ask what they recommend.
2. Look through your Medicare Summary Notices, or your account on Medicare.gov, for any tests, services, or equipment you didn't receive.
3. Contact the Senior Medicare Patrol at 1-877-808-2468 ([smpresource.org](https://smpresource.org/)). They help people spot and report Medicare billing fraud for free.
4. Report the call to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).
5. If you also shared your Social Security number or bank details, call your bank and think about freezing your credit with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Medicare ever call you?

Medicare says it will never call you to sell you anything or come to your home, and it only asks for personal information in limited cases, like returning a call you made. It won't call to confirm your Medicare number. If you're not sure about a call, hang up and dial 1-800-MEDICARE yourself.

### When is Medicare open enrollment for 2027 coverage?

It runs from October 15 through December 7, 2026. Any changes you make during that window take effect on January 1, 2027.

### Is a free genetic test covered by Medicare a scam?

If it was offered over the phone, at your door, or at a health fair, it's very likely a scam. Medicare covers genetic testing when your own doctor orders it because it's medically necessary.

### Can someone steal my identity with just my Medicare number?

They can use it to bill Medicare in your name, which can cause problems with your records and future coverage. It's worth guarding as carefully as your Social Security number.

### How do I stop Medicare robocalls?

Register your number at donotcall.gov, avoid pressing any buttons to "opt out," and just hang up. If the same callers keep trying, report them to the FTC.
