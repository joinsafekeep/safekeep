**Quick answer:** Medicare open enrollment runs October 15 to December 7, 2026, and scam calls surge with it. The simplest rule: Medicare will not call, text, or email you to confirm your Medicare number, issue a new card, or sell you a plan. If someone asks for your Medicare number, offers something "free," or says you'll lose benefits unless you act today, hang up and call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227).

## Why do Medicare scams spike during open enrollment?

Because real and fake calls arrive at the same time. Open enrollment for 2027 coverage opened on October 15, 2026, and legitimate insurers and Medicare Advantage plans contact beneficiaries during these weeks too, which makes impostors harder to spot ([TheStreet](https://thestreet.com/personal-finance/medicare-scam-calls-open-enrollment-2026)). Scammers lean on the deadline to create urgency and pose as government officials offering card updates, genetic tests, or warnings that coverage will be canceled.

The calls start early. Beneficiaries reported getting Medicare-related calls several times a day by late August, even on the Do Not Call registry ([Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/dont-waste-your-money/they-call-15-times-a-day-seniors-inundated-with-medicare-calls-ahead-of-open-enrollment)).

## What are the most common Medicare scam calls?

**1. "We need to confirm your Medicare number."** Medicare already knows your number and will not call, text, or email asking you to confirm it ([Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/dont-waste-your-money/they-call-15-times-a-day-seniors-inundated-with-medicare-calls-ahead-of-open-enrollment)). Your Medicare number is as valuable to a thief as your Social Security number, because it can be used to bill Medicare for services you never received.

**2. "Your new or upgraded Medicare card is ready."** Offers of a new, plastic, or "upgraded" card are a warning sign. There is no fee and no phone verification for a Medicare card.

**3. "Free genetic test" or cheek swab.** Scammers offer free DNA or cancer screening tests by phone, at health fairs, or at your door. What they actually want is your Medicare number so they can bill for expensive tests you didn't need ([MedicareFAQ](https://www.medicarefaq.com/blog/7-medicare-scams-targeting-seniors/)).

**4. "Free" braces, devices, or a "Medicare kit."** Calls and texts offering a free back brace, knee brace, or medical kit are a common front for equipment billing fraud. More than 40 health systems recently warned patients about a fake "MyChart Medicare Kit" message ([Scripps News, citing Becker's Healthcare](https://www.scrippsnews.com/dont-waste-your-money/they-call-15-times-a-day-seniors-inundated-with-medicare-calls-ahead-of-open-enrollment)). If you need equipment, your own doctor will order it.

**5. "This plan is endorsed by Medicare."** No private plan is endorsed or preferred by Medicare, and insurance agents are not allowed to say they represent Medicare. Anyone claiming either is breaking the rules or running a scam.

**6. "You'll lose your benefits if you don't switch today."** You have until December 7 to make changes, and doing nothing does not cancel your coverage. A caller who insists you decide on this call is pressuring you, not helping you.

## How can you tell a real Medicare call from a scam?

Ask yourself three questions:

- **Did I contact them first?** Under Medicare marketing rules, plans and agents generally may not cold-call you unless you've asked them to or are already a member.
- **Are they asking for my Medicare number, Social Security number, or bank details?** Hang up.
- **Are they rushing me?** Real coverage decisions can wait a day while you check.

When in doubt, end the call and dial 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) yourself, or compare plans directly at [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/). For free, unbiased help choosing a plan, contact your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP).

## What should you do if you gave out your Medicare number?

1. **Call 1-800-MEDICARE** (1-800-633-4227) to report it and ask what protections are available.
2. **Check your Medicare Summary Notices** or your Medicare.gov account for services, tests, or equipment you never received.
3. **Contact the Senior Medicare Patrol** at 1-877-808-2468 ([smpresource.org](https://smpresource.org/)) for free help spotting and reporting billing fraud.
4. **Report the call** to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).
5. **If you also shared a Social Security or bank number,** call your bank and consider a credit freeze with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Medicare ever call you?

Medicare generally won't call unless you've asked it to, and it will never call to confirm your Medicare number or sell you a plan. If you're unsure, hang up and call 1-800-MEDICARE yourself.

### When is Medicare open enrollment for 2027 coverage?

October 15 through December 7, 2026. Changes made in this window take effect January 1, 2027.

### Is a free genetic test covered by Medicare a scam?

If it was offered by phone, at your door, or at a health fair, treat it as a scam. Medicare covers genetic testing only when your own doctor orders it as medically necessary.

### Can someone steal my identity with just my Medicare number?

They can bill Medicare in your name, which can affect your records and future coverage. Guard it the way you guard your Social Security number.

### How do I stop Medicare robocalls?

Register at donotcall.gov, don't press any buttons to "opt out," and simply hang up. Report persistent callers to the FTC.
